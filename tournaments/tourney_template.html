The chip-value curve E = Sβ, β = −log₂C

A player's equity as a function of his stack fraction S, for a chance-of-doubling C. C = 0.5 is exactly linear (chips = equity). Skilled players (C > 0.5) sit above the line — a skill premium — yet the curve is concave, so each marginal chip is still worth less than the last. Losing players (C < 0.5) sit below and should court variance.

Rebuy / add-on value (ch. 28, on the doubling-up engine)

Case 2: 1,000,000 chips in play, $90,000 pool, a 1500-chip add-on. Its cash value as your stack grows, for three skill levels. The discounted add-on ($100) clears its cost all the way to a huge stack; the at-par rebuy ($150) has a much earlier break-even.

Reproduces the book: at 6000 chips E = $953, at 7500 chips E = $1,162, so the $100 add-on (worth ≈ $209) is automatic; at-par break-even (C = 0.51) sits near a 8,325 stack.
