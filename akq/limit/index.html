The game

A three-card deck: Ace, King, Queen. Each player antes into a pot of P and is dealt one card. One half street of limit betting (bet size 1): X checks in the dark, then Y bets or checks; facing a bet, X calls or folds. High card wins the showdown. Holding the King you know the opponent has the nuts or nothing — the deck itself creates perfectly polarized card removal, which the symmetric [0,1] games can't show.

Dominance does most of the work

Before any mixing, pure logic deletes most of the game:

HoldingX (out of position)Y (bettor)
Acecall foldbet check
Kingmix call / foldcheck bet
Queenfold callmix bluff / check

Never fold the nuts, never call with a dead hand, never value-bet the middle card (it only gets called by the Ace), never check the nuts. What survives is a 2×2 game — Y: bluff queens or not; X: call with kings or not — which the book shows is exactly Cops & Robbers from ch. 10.

Y's bluff frequency b* = 1/(P+1) falls as the pot grows; X's calling frequency c* = (P−1)/(P+1) rises toward always-call; the game value (P−1)/(6(P+1)) climbs toward its ceiling of 1/6. Everything kinks at P = 1: below it a bluff loses even when every King folds (half of X's remaining deck is Aces), so nobody bluffs, nobody calls, and the game is worth zero.

Why α is everywhere

Y bets every Ace and α of his Queens — bluffs are a fixed fraction of value bets, chosen so a King gains nothing by calling. X folds α of the hands that beat a bluff — chosen so a Queen gains nothing by bluffing. The same constant prices both sides of the bet, here with a three-card deck, in the [0,1] games with a continuum, and in real poker with 1,326 combos.
