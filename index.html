Mathematics of Poker, Visualized

Exact computational companions to Bill Chen & Jerrod Ankenman's The Mathematics of Poker — every game family in the book re-solved from scratch (closed forms where they exist, exact enumeration and linear programming where they don't), every printed number reproduced or flagged as an erratum, and the "exhaustive analyses" the book alludes to but never prints, actually carried out. Each page below is a self-contained interactive explorer; each folder in the repository has the solvers and a full report.

The game families

ch. 13 · the gateway
The AKQ Game, Limit
Three cards, one bet: poker's simplest nontrivial form, where α = 1/(P+1) prices every bluff and every call.
Game value (P−1)/6(P+1), frozen below P=1 — and verified against a saddle-point solve.

The AKQ Game, No-Limit
Free the bet size and solve at 300 stack depths: the King's blocking bet, the merged raising range, and four regimes — three in closed form.
Position's value freezes at S* = 0.5254 pots: a 1-pot and a 100-pot stack are worth exactly the same.
ch. 11, 16–18
The [0,1] Games
Hands as a continuum, from von Neumann's 1944 model through bets, raises, check-raises, and the infinite raising ladder — with an exact sequence-form LP where the book stops.
Game #11's x₁ is a band, not a line; game #12's unpublished value: 0.06696 at P=2.
ch. 12
The Jam-or-Fold Game
Preflop NLHE, shove or fold, solved exactly at 1,379 stack sizes from exact equities (812,175 matchups × 1.7M boards) — the book's promised "exhaustive analysis."
ATs joins AA at S = 833.93; one book erratum (A5s vs AA = 0.13307); attacker favored only below S* = 7.797.

Multi-Street Games
Bet sizing as an optimization: √2−1 on one street, and across many, the book's most famous result — the geometric growth of pot.
The $185 case study: sizing alone swings the game from −$0.51 to +$0.60.

Beyond the book

  • Jam-or-fold: the full 1→2,500-bb equilibrium chronology — 478 transitions, 21 strictly-verified non-monotonic hands, ATs exiled twice and mirrored exactly by the defender's AKs calls; game-value crossover at S* = 7.797.
  • NL AKQ: three exact regime laws, a C¹-smooth phase boundary at s = √2−1, and the self-referential plateau: depth stops mattering when the stack covers Y's largest equilibrium bet.
  • [0,1] games: the sequence-form LP settles the open x₁ question (the optimal set pins aggregates to 10⁻⁵ while leaving every hand's assignment free) and computes game #12's never-published value curve.
  • Multi-street: the auction-game limit verified at M = 1000 streets; Figure 19.1 completed for 2–5 streets.
  • Tournaments: the chip-value curve E = Sβ charted continuously across skill; rebuy strategy on the doubling-up engine.

Errata found in the book

  • p. 130 (ch. 12): A5s vs AA equity is exactly 0.13307, not 0.13331 — which moves the forced mixing at S = 833 from ATs onto A5s.
  • p. 144 (ch. 13): the bluff ratio at P = 1.1 is 10/21 (= 1/(P+1)), not the printed "10/11".
  • ch. 22: two numeric solves of ⟨e−αx⟩ = 1 are imprecise: the sit-and-go α is 0.000606 (printed ≈0.000632, and once as "0.00632"), the winner-take-all α is 0.016153 (printed 0.01683); its σ = 14.036 forgot to subtract μ² (exact 14.0). Conclusions unaffected.
  • ch. 24, Ex. 24.2: the game-selection cutoff: the derivation correctly reaches 2c = $19,295, then prints c = $19,295. The cutoff is c = $9,647.50 (verified by utility crossover).
  • ch. 25: the backer's share of the worked agreement is $1,546.64 ($1,547.64 printed — a $1 slip).
  • ch. 27: Landrum-Burns P3 must be $1,166.67 (printed $1,066.67) to sum to the prize pool; the evening tournament's variance multiple is 53.263, not 53.6456.
  • ch. 29, Ex. 29.2 (dry side pot): the reported equilibrium x ≈ 0.2848 does not satisfy the book's own indifference system and admits profitable deviations; the true equilibrium is x = 0.5178, y₁ = 0.2589, bluff band 0.7589 ± 0.0242.

Each is verified in the corresponding folder's solver with the book's own equations; "erratum" here means the printed number is inconsistent with the book's own stated model, not a disagreement of modeling taste.

Method, throughout

Exact methods over approximation: full enumeration for equities (133,784,560 seven-card hands verified against known frequency counts), linear programming with strong-duality checks for equilibria, closed forms re-derived and verified by independent numerics everywhere they exist, and strict EV-margin verification so LP degeneracy can't masquerade as a finding. Every explorer is a single dependency-free HTML page (vanilla JS + Canvas) that works offline. Built with the help of Claude (Anthropic), reading the book chapter by chapter.
