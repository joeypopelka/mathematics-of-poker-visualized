Value of the game to Y  vs. pot

Ex-showdown expectation to Y: G = α(1−α)²/[2(1+α)] (Chapter 18). It is humped — rising then falling, peaking near P≈2.6 at ≈0.057 — and → 0 as P→∞ (the no-fold Game #4 value: with an infinite pot nobody folds, so the one-bet full-street lead is worth nothing). The dot marks the current pot.

How to read it

The one lever is α = 1/(P+1), the “golden ratio of poker.” It is the fraction of hands the bettor bluffs relative to his value bets, and the fraction the caller must fold to stay unexploitable.

Value bets and bluffs are balanced so the opponent is exactly indifferent to calling — that indifference is the solution.

Bigger pot → smaller α → more action. With more dead money to win, folding gets expensive, so both players bet, call, and bluff a wider slice of hands. As P → ∞ every threshold marches to 1: it collapses to the no-fold games of Chapter 16, where everyone value-bets and calls with everything.

As P → 0 the pot isn’t worth fighting for, α → 1, and all the regions vanish into a single check-down.

Notice the alignment of the gridlines: y₁ always lands at the midpoint of X’s betting and calling thresholds — Y value-bets everything X would have, plus half the hands X would merely call with.

Model & closed-form thresholds from Bill Chen & Jerrod Ankenman, The Mathematics of Poker, Example 17.1 (“[0,1] Game #9”). Solution: x₁=(1−α)²/(1+α), y₁=(1−α)/(1+α), x₁*=y₁*=1−α, y₀=(1+α²)/(1+α), x₀=1−αx₁. Verified against the book’s P=4 worked example (α=1/5 → x₁=8/15, y₁=2/3, x₁*=4/5, y₀=13/15, x₀=67/75).
