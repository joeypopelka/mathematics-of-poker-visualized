Chapters 11 & 16–18 of Chen & Ankenman's The Mathematics of Poker: hands uniform on [0,1] (0 = the nuts), pot P, limit bets of 1 — poker reduced to pure betting structure. One interactive page per game, from von Neumann's 1944 half-street model to the unlimited-raise ladder, plus an exact sequence-form LP that settles what the book leaves open.

One chart: what each strategic option is worth

Exact game value to Y (ex-showdown) by pot size, all four finite-pot games (sequence-form LP; values_fp.json). Dashed: the P→∞ no-fold limits. Note the strict ordering #9 < #11 < #12 < #10 at every pot beyond the tiny ones: Y's raise option (#9→#10) is worth the most; giving X the check-raise (#10→#11) or the re-raise war (#10→#12) hands part of it back. #9's curve is the odd one — it humps near P ≈ 2.6 and falls to zero, because with one bet and no raises, a huge pot makes the bettor's edge evaporate (its no-fold limit is Game #4's value: zero).
