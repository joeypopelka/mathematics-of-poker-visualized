[0,1] Game #11 two bets, with check-raising — the strategy structure, made live

One full street, two bets left, and now X may check-raise. Hands are uniform on [0, 1], 0 the nuts. This is where the book stops giving a full closed-form solution. The Y-side value thresholds are exact (Eq. 17.13); everything else is now solved exactly by sequence-form LP — which settled the old open question: x₁ is not a line. The equilibrium pins X's aggregate frequencies to five decimal places while leaving each individual hand's assignment free; the X-side drawn here is the canonical (monotone, book-style) representative of that optimal set. Two new ideas appear: X check-raises among his strongest hands, and Y's value bets split into bet-call vs bet-fold to a check-raise.

What check-raise changes

The value regions are what move; everything else cascades off them. The book solves the Y-side in closed form — the key result is Eq. 17.13:

y₁ = (1−α) / [ 1 + α + (1−α₂)R ]

In Game #10 (no check-raise) the ratio R = (1−α₂)/2 linked an X threshold to a Y threshold (y₂ = R·x₁). With check-raise it links two Y thresholds instead: y₂ = R·y₁. The recurrence shifts from X→Y to Y→Y.

X check-raises his best hands. On his strongest region he is indifferent between check-raising and bet-calling, and he adds a band of check-raise bluffs among his give-up hands (◆). Betting a little of his strength as a trap lets him punish Y's bets.

Y bets tighter, raises looser. Fearing the check-raise, Y value-bets a narrower range after X checks, and splits it into bet-call [0, y₂*] and bet-fold [y₂*, y₁] — folding α₂ of his value bets to a check-raise (y₂* = (1−α₂)y₁).

The x₁\* = y₁\* symmetry breaks. In #9 and #10 those cutoffs coincided; here they separate, because X's post-check decision is no longer a clean call/fold — he can check-raise. That's the structural signature of this game.

How these numbers are computed — the sequence-form LP, and what it found. The Y-side value thresholds (y₂, y₂*, y₁, y₀) are the book's exact closed forms (Eq. 17.13). The rest come from an exact sequence-form LP (Koller–Megiddo–von Stengel; seqlp.py), gate-validated by reproducing Games #9 and #10's closed forms (x₁ = 5/21, x₂* = 4/21, x₁* = 68/105 at P=2) and the no-fold #7 limit. The LP settled the question CFR could not: there is no crisp x₁. At P=2 the optimal set pins X's total bet mass to 0.28763 ± 0.00001 and total aggressive mass (bet + check-raise) to 0.37533 ± 0.00001, yet every individual hand's action probability can range over [0,1] — rigorously measured by maximizing/minimizing each hand's bet frequency over the optimal face. The same freedom holds for Y's give-up zone (call/raise-bluff/fold assignment on ≈[0.55, 0.85]). The thresholds drawn here are the canonical monotone representative (aggression pushed lexicographically toward stronger hands — the selection that reproduces the book's own #9/#10 thresholds exactly). Under it, x₁* ≈ 0.650 ≠ y₁* ≈ 0.667 (the book's predicted split, confirmed), y₁* = y₂# (a representative with no raise-bluffs exists in the optimal face), and the old CFR "blur" at x₁ stands revealed as genuine equilibrium-set structure, not solver noise. The game value is exact: G(Y) = 0.062142 at P=2 (N→∞ converged), confirming the region-sum formula.
